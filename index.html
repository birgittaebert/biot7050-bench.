Codon Recoding Bench

Pick codons to remove from a coding sequence set, apply synonymous replacements, and see what the rewrite costs — edits required, and the regulatory signals you break along the way.

1 · Sequence set

For the exercise, load the real E. coli K-12 MG1655 CDS file from NCBI — cds_from_genomic.fna. Use the file button rather than copy-paste; the whole genome's 4,000-odd coding sequences load fine. The demo records are synthetic ORFs built with E. coli-like codon usage — realistic in composition, but not real genes.

2 · Codons to remove

Each cell shows the codon, its amino acid, and how many times it occurs in your set. ATG and TGG are locked — they are the only codons for Met and Trp, so neither can be removed. At least one stop codon must remain.

Extrapolate to a whole genome

Your sample gives a codon frequency. Scale it to a genome to see how big the build actually is. Four genes is far too small a sample to trust — stop-codon usage especially varies between genes — so load at least 50 real CDSs before you quote a genome-wide number.
